How every figure here is calculated

Nothing on this site is a judgement, so every number has a definition. These are the definitions.

The scores

Momentum (0–100)

A cross-sectional percentile, not a return. Each name is ranked against its peer group on relative-strength level and trend, 9- and 20-day EMA slopes, and price against its 50-day average; the composite is then expressed as a position from 0 to 100.

50 is the median, not a neutral return. In a falling market a score of 90 means fell least, not rose. This is the single most misread number on the site, which is why the return column sits next to it everywhere.

Stocks are scored against the whole 1,931-name universe; funds are scored against the other funds on their board. The two numbers are not comparable with each other.

RS rank

A position, not a score, and it runs the other way: #1 is the strongest name we cover, so lower is better. It ranks a stock's price against the S&P 500 over the past few weeks.

A name can hold high momentum and a middling RS rank at once. Momentum rewards a trend that is improving; RS rewards one that is already ahead.

Durability

The share of the last 60 trading days a fund spent in the top 10% of its board. The average fund scores about 10% by construction. It separates a fund that has led for months from one that led yesterday.

8/21

A yes/no: the price closed above its 8-day EMA, with the 8-day above the 21-day. A short-term trend check, nothing more.

Δ rank

How far a row has moved on the column you are currently sorting by, against the previous session and the one five sessions back. Change the sort and the column follows it — it is not a fixed measure.

Ties take the minimum rank, so equal values share a position rather than being split arbitrarily. Without that, two funds on identical scores could show a rank change that no price caused.

The returns

Windows are trading sessions, not calendar days

The 20-session figure compares the latest close with 2026-09-30 and the 60-session figure with 2026-08-04 — about a month and nearer three months than two.

Each is a close against a single earlier day, so a window that happens to start on a spike or a trough can read very differently from the trend. From high is the check to run when a green return sits beside a deep drawdown.

vs SPY and vs QQQ

The benchmark's return over the same window, subtracted. Worth reading together: beating the S&P while lagging the Nasdaq says the move belongs to the broad market rather than to the fund.

The regime line

A cross-sectional score cannot tell you what the market did, only where something stands inside it. So the board states the benchmark's direction in words before any score is read, and counts the rows where a good rank and a falling price disagree. It switches at ±2% on the benchmark's 20-session return.

The news

Ranked by separate sources, never by article count

Stories are ordered by how many independent outlets carried them. Counting articles instead would measure syndication — one wire story reprinted forty times would outrank a scoop that four newsrooms chased themselves.

What a story is about is decided by a program

Cashtags first, then full company names, then unambiguous first words — and the last of those only when market vocabulary sits nearby, because without that constraint a story about a politician matched a media company and a Nobel prize matched a staffing firm.

Research houses have to be the subject of a story, not the source of a view in it. "Goldman says" is not a Goldman story, and counting it as one put a bank at the top of a board it had nothing to do with.

Movers come from our own closes

The biggest movers are taken from the prices, not from coverage. The move is the fact; a headline is colour attached where one exists. "No coverage" against a double-digit move is itself worth reading, and general market feeds routinely miss small and mid-caps entirely.

Sessions, not filing dates

US cash equities open at 13:30 UTC. Anything filed before that belongs to the session that just finished, and weekends roll back to the Friday. Without this, a recap written at 03:00 about yesterday's close would be filed under a session that has not happened yet.

The value chains

357 companies mapped by hand across 107 stages in 18 themes. A chain is ordered the way value is actually added, from raw inputs through to whoever sells the finished thing, because money rarely moves into a theme evenly — it moves into a stage of one.

This mapping is ours. It matches no index and no fund's published holdings, and it reflects a judgement about what belongs where. Where a stage carries too few scored names to read, the page says so rather than printing a number.

Where the data comes from

End-of-day prices and fundamentals from public market data; fund holdings as published by the issuers; the earnings calendar from Nasdaq's public feed; news from publisher RSS and an aggregator, with the publisher recovered per story so that one request yields many separate sources rather than one.

Everything is rebuilt each weeknight after the US close. The newest figures on the site are the close of 2026-10-28. Nothing updates intraday.
