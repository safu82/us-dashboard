About Overnight Arc

A record of what happened on the US market last session, and of where money is moving, rebuilt from scratch every weeknight by a program rather than written by a desk.

Who publishes it

Overnight Arc is published by Insight Arc Solutions, a company registered in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. It is built and maintained by one person. There is no newsroom, no analyst team, and no third-party research behind any figure on the site.

In practice that means nothing here is chosen by hand. The same scoring runs against every name in the universe every night, and no story is promoted because somebody found it interesting. A research desk can exercise judgement where the numbers are misleading; this cannot. That is a real limitation in some places and the entire point in others.

What it does

Every weeknight after the US close, a scan reads end-of-day prices for 1,931 US-listed stocks and the funds that hold them, scores each one against its own peer group, and writes the result here. The same run reads the market wires, works out which company each story is about, and ranks the day's news by how many separate outlets carried each item.

The four boards show the latest session. Alongside them sit permanent pages: one for each of 54 funds, 24 country ETFs and 18 themes, and one for every session, written the night it happens and never rewritten.

What it never does

No recommendations

Nothing here is a view on whether to buy or sell anything. A ranking is a statement about where something sits against its peers, which is not the same as a reason to own it.

Nothing is personalised

The site does not know who you are, what you hold, or what you are trying to achieve, and nothing on it is adjusted for any of that.

Nothing is sold, sponsored or paid for

No advertising, no sponsored placements, no affiliate links, no paid inclusion. Nobody can buy their way onto a board, and no ordering here was chosen by a person — every rank is computed.

No opinion in the ordering

Stories are ordered by how many independent outlets carried them. Companies are ordered by where they sit against their own peers. Earnings are ordered by size. None of those orderings is a judgement.

What it is not good at

Five things to know before relying on anything here:

It is delayed, never live

Everything is end-of-day. The newest figures on the site are the close of 2026-10-28. Nothing updates during market hours.

A score cannot tell you what the market did

Momentum is cross-sectional: it ranks a name against others, so in a market where everything is falling the leader is simply whatever fell least. That is why the return column sits beside it and why the board states the direction of the benchmark in words before any score is read.

The value chains are ours, and are hand-made

357 companies mapped across 107 stages in 18 themes, by hand. They match no index and no fund's holdings, they reflect a judgement about what belongs where, and they will sometimes be wrong or out of date.

News matching is automated and makes mistakes

Working out which company a headline is about is done by a program. It has reported a Canadian newspaper as an insurance company, an Australian mining junior as a US chemicals firm, and a bank quoted in somebody else's story as the subject of it. Each of those was found and fixed; others will be there now.

Listed in the US is not the same as American

Everything here is US-listed and priced in dollars. Taiwan Semiconductor, ASML and Novo Nordisk are all in the maps.

Corrections

Mistakes get fixed and the fix gets explained. If a figure looks wrong, or a company is in the wrong place, or a story is attributed to the wrong name, write to contact@overnightarc.com and it will be looked at. Specifics help more than impressions — the page and the number are enough.

Not advice

This is educational research. It is not investment advice, it is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security, and it is not personalised to anyone. Insight Arc Solutions is not a licensed investment adviser and nothing here should be treated as the opinion of one. Do your own work, and take advice from somebody qualified to give it.
