# The Hike Nobody Panicked About

*Weekly US market letter — week of September 25, 2026*

The Fed raised rates this week. Not a cut, not a pause, but a hike under new chair Kevin Warsh, and the bond market answered by shoving the 10-year Treasury yield to a 19-year high. The textbook says stocks fall when money gets more expensive. Instead the S&P 500 rose 1.2% on the week, and the money that moved didn't run to cash. It crowded into cancer diagnostics, biotech, and a fresh wave of chip names. Everything below rests on our daily ranking of about 1,900 US stocks, and this week the board and the bond market told two very different stories.

## 1. The lead: a rate hike the market shrugged off

Warsh's FOMC hiked, Treasury Secretary Bessent said he has "great confidence" in the chair, and yields did the rest of the talking. The 10-year sits at 5.18%, up 24 basis points on the week; the 2-year is 4.87%, up 20. The 2s10s spread is a positive 31 basis points and steepening, which matters: the long end is selling off faster than the short end, so the bond market is pricing more inflation and term premium, not a recession. Fed funds now top out at 3.63%, against CPI still running 3.4% year over year.

Higher long yields are supposed to punish expensive, long-duration stocks first, the growth names whose value sits years out. That's the part worth sitting with. The Fed tightened, borrowing costs jumped to levels not seen since the mid-2000s, and the index still closed green while leadership rotated toward companies with earnings you can touch today. The market isn't fighting the Fed. It's repricing which businesses deserve the money now that the money costs more.

## 3. Oil, the dollar, and the world into our open

Asia traded into the US hike with more nerve than you'd expect. Taiwan's TAIEX rose 1.8% and Korea's KOSPI jumped 2.7%, the memory-and-chip complex firming right before Micron reports next week, a quiet vote of confidence in the semi cycle. Japan's Nikkei added 2.1% with no sign of a carry-trade unwind, though USD/JPY, up 1.7% on the week to 157.2, has ticked back about 1% since our close. That's mild yen firming into Monday, worth a glance, not a worry. Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped 1%, the one soft spot, telling you China demand is still lagging.

The dollar index rose 0.8% to 101, partly the rate hike and partly a low-grade safety bid, and a firmer dollar is a headwind for US multinationals and anything priced in it. Which brings up the week's loudest commodity move: Brent crude fell 6.2% to 97.44, with no Middle-East supply scare attached. Cheaper oil is disinflationary and a straight gift to fuel-heavy businesses, part of why airlines lit up the board (more on that below). Copper rose 2.3%, so the growth barometer isn't flashing red, and Bitcoin's 3.9% gain says risk appetite is intact.

## 5. Theme of the week: quantum computing

Set aside the hype and quantum computing is a supply chain like any other, and money in a supply chain flows to the chokepoint, the one link everybody needs no matter who wins. Our theme momentum score jumped to 77 from 67 a week ago, so this chain is heating up. Here's how it's built.

At the front are the components — the lasers, photonics and precision test gear from Coherent, FormFactor, Lumentum and MKS. Behind them, the big-tech machine builders: Alphabet, Honeywell, IBM, Microsoft, Nvidia. Then the pure-plays — IonQ, D-Wave, Quantum Computing Inc., Rigetti — the lottery tickets betting on specific qubit designs. And last, the quantum-safe security layer (Arqit, SEALSQ) racing to build encryption that a working quantum computer couldn't crack.

The tell is where the momentum sits. Components score 78 and pure-plays 78, big tech 77, but the security layer lags at 64. In plain terms, the money is on the machines and the parts that go in them, not yet on the shield against them. The single strongest node is FormFactor, momentum 92 and ranked #141 of our whole universe, the picks-and-shovels play. When you don't know which horse wins the race, you sell every rider a saddle, and the saddle-makers are winning.

One caution the same theme hands you: Xanadu, a quantum name, fell 33% this week. And the "cheapest on growth" screen flags Quantum Computing Inc. at a price-to-sales ratio of 206 against revenue growth of **9,000%** year over year — numbers so extreme they tell you this is a story stock priced for a future that may or may not arrive. That's a screen, not a suggestion. Treat this whole link as a lesson in how a value chain concentrates, not a shopping list.

Run the fear panel and the answer is that the hike didn't scare anyone. The VIX is asleep under 15, gold actually fell 2.3% (no safe-haven bid), junk bonds are down a trivial 0.8%, and the dollar's rise looks more like a rate story than a panic. Credit spreads are unavailable this week, so I won't pretend to read them. Two or more of those gauges turning would mean the tape was hedging; none of them blinked. As a fear check, this scare was noise.

The genuine caution isn't fear, it's breadth. Only **46% of stocks sit above their 200-day**, and just 30% are above their 50-day, with zero new 52-week highs against one new low. The index is a fifth of a percent from its August record, but fewer and fewer stocks are doing the lifting. A narrow rally can run for a long time; it just leaves less room for error when leadership stumbles.

- **Base case (55%):** the market grinds sideways-to-higher, health care and select semis keep leading, and yields stabilize after the hike. Narrow but intact.
- **Bull case (20%):** Micron's print and the memory cycle confirm the chip move, breadth broadens beyond health care, and the hike becomes the "one and done" the steepening curve half-expects.
- **Bear case (25%):** long yields keep climbing past the market's comfort, the thin participation finally cracks, and the handful of leaders roll over with nothing underneath to catch the index.

Stance: constructive but selective. Respect the trends in front of you — health care leadership, firming semis — buy strength on pauses rather than chasing the 20% weekly spikes, and keep your stops honest while breadth stays this thin.

*Methodology. The Algo is a long-only, rules-based paper strategy on a $50,000 notional sleeve, trading the same ~1,900-stock universe. A name is bought only if it clears every gate: its sector ranks near the top of the board with broad participation, earnings are growing year-over-year in both revenue and profit, it isn't already held, and it's past a 21-day cooldown. Qualifying setups are sorted into four conviction tiers by how many independent signals fire — from T1 (multiple 'strong' chart patterns, risked at 1.5% of the sleeve) down to T4 (relative-strength acceleration, 0.5%). Size is set by risk, not dollars: each trade is sized so a stop 2×ATR below entry costs only that tier's risk budget, capped at $2,000–$6,000. Exits are mechanical — scale out a third at +2R and a third at +4R (1R = the initial stop distance), trail the remainder by 2×ATR, book some after a +25% spike, and time out positions left flat after 25 trading days. New entries pause if the book is down 2% on the day or 8% from its peak. Signals are generated after the close and filled the next session at the live price plus 0.15% slippage. These are educational paper results — not advice, and not a live account.*

## 11. Glossary

- **Our rank / the board:** we rank every US stock we track by relative strength, #1 strongest. "#8 in our system" means 8th-strongest of about 1,900.
- **Relative strength (RS):** how a stock performs against the rest of the market, not on its own. Rising RS = outperforming.
- **Moving averages (20/50/200-day):** the average price over that many days — the trend lines. A healthy uptrend has price above a rising 20-day, itself above the 200-day. "Extended" means far above the 20-day and prone to a pullback.
- **VIX:** the market's fear gauge. Below 20 is calm, above 20 is stress.
- **Credit spreads:** the extra yield investors demand to hold risky corporate bonds over safe government bonds. Widening is an early warning; tight is calm.
- **Treasury yields / 2s10s:** what the government pays to borrow for 10 years versus 2. The 2s10s spread is the 10-year minus the 2-year; a rising 10-year pressures expensive, long-duration stocks like chips and growth.
- **Breadth:** how many stocks are participating. Thin breadth (few above their 200-day, no new highs) means a narrow rally.
- **High-yield / junk bonds:** bonds from riskier companies. Investors sell them first when scared, so a falling junk price is an early credit-stress signal.
- **Safe havens (gold, the dollar):** where money hides when afraid. A rally in them during a shock means fear is real; flat through a scary headline means the market shrugged.
