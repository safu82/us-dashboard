*Issue · 2026-09-11 · a weekly read built on ranking ~1,900 US stocks every day*

Under it, something less calm: not a single stock we track finished the week above its 50-day moving average, and the odds of a Fed rate *hike* on September 16 are running over 60%. Money didn't panic out of the market — advancers still beat decliners 3 to 1 — but it thinned out badly, and it's parking in strange corners while it waits for Wednesday.

## 1. The lead: the market is pricing a rate hike, not a cut

For most of this cycle the argument was about *when* the Fed cuts. This week the argument flipped. Coverage went one direction all week — odds of a hike at the September 16 FOMC surged past 60%, and a wave of "no cut next week" pieces followed. The trigger is inflation refusing to behave: wholesale prices (PPI) picked up in August, gasoline is pushing consumer prices higher, and Friday's CPI landed as the most-watched print in months.

The board reads at CPI 3.4% year over year, still comfortably above the Fed's target, and the Fed funds upper target sits at 3.63%. When a central bank that markets assumed was done tightening starts sounding like it might go the other way, long-duration stocks (the expensive, growth-heavy names whose value sits far in the future) get repriced first. That's a big part of why the whole market slid under its 50-day this week even though the index barely moved. The average stock took the hit while a handful of index heavyweights held the tape up.

What it means for your money: don't fight the front end. Until the Fed meeting clears, the tape is going to treat every inflation-flavored headline as a reason to sell duration and hide.

## 2. Oil did the heavy lifting on inflation

The single loudest cross-asset move this week was Brent crude, up 8.7% over the US week to 104.6. That's the direct line behind the "gasoline is boosting CPI" story — an oil move that size feeds straight into the wholesale and pump numbers the Fed is staring at.

An 8.7% jump in a week usually rides a supply shock or a Middle-East headline, and it does two things at once. It stiffens the inflation case, which is bad for the rate-hike odds and bad for growth stocks, and it hands the energy complex a real tailwind. You'll see that second part show up in our paper portfolio's book below. It's leaning heavily into energy, and this is why.

Copper, the other side of the commodity story, slipped 0.7% and Hang Seng fell 3.3% on soft China demand. So this isn't a broad "everything's booming" commodity surge. It's oil specifically, which is the inflation-unfriendly kind.

## 3. Rates rose, the world stayed mixed

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed 10 basis points on the week to 4.77%, the 2-year jumped 14bp to 4.34%, and the 2s10s spread sits at a positive 43bp and steepening. Rising yields with a steepening curve is the bond market saying "growth and inflation, both a little hotter," and it's a headwind for anything priced on far-off earnings.

Overseas, the tape didn't line up neatly. Korea's KOSPI ran +3.3% on the week, and that's the memory-chip tell: Samsung and SK Hynix leading is the read-through to Micron and US memory, a rare bright spot for the semis while Taiwan (TAIEX -0.8%) and the AI-logic side stayed flat. Japan's Nikkei fell 1.6% while the yen firmed (USD/JPY down to 153.6, a 1.4% yen gain). Yen strength that fast is the carry-trade gauge worth watching, not a full unwind here, but the direction that precedes global risk-off if it accelerates. Bitcoin, the weekend risk proxy, slid 3.1%.

Add it up: the world traded cautiously into a US week that was already bracing for the Fed. Nothing broke. Nothing celebrated.

## 5. Theme of the week: cybersecurity, layer by layer

Think of a company's digital defenses the way you'd think about protecting a building. First you decide who's allowed through the door (identity). Then you guard each room and every laptop inside (endpoint), the hallways connecting them (network), the offsite storage unit (cloud), the mailroom where strangers send things in (email and web), the filing cabinets and their backups (data), and the blueprints the whole place is built from (code). Finally you put guards in a control room watching the cameras (the security operations center, or SOC), and you hire people to run all of it (services).

Our platform maps the cybersecurity value chain across those layers. This is a curated teaching spotlight, not a call on the sector and not a claim we cover the whole market. It's one lesson in how a chain works.

Two things are worth teaching here. First, notice how a few names appear in link after link — Microsoft shows up in identity, endpoint and email; Palo Alto in network, cloud and the SOC. That's the land-grab logic of security: whoever owns the control room wants to sell you the door locks too, because one vendor watching every layer sees threats the point-products miss. Money in this business flows toward the platform that spans the most links, and cost flows away from the single-purpose tool that only guards one room.

Second, the standout our screen flagged sits at the unglamorous end of the chain: **CACI International, on price-to-sales of 1.4 with revenue growing 18% year over year.** That's the services layer — the people and contractors, heavy on government work, who actually operate the defenses. It's a screen, not a recommendation, and a reminder that the cheapest growth in a hot theme often hides in the least exciting link. The control-room software gets the magazine covers; the folks running it get overlooked.

One live tie: our paper portfolio was stopped out of ESTC this week (down 11.1%), a name in the detection/SOC layer. Even inside a structurally growing theme, the individual charts weren't spared the broad breakdown. A good long-term story and a good current tape are two different questions.

## 11. Glossary

- **Our rank / the board:** we rank every US stock we track by relative strength, #1 strongest. "#4 in our system" = 4th-strongest of ~1,900.
- **Relative strength (RS):** performance versus the rest of the market. Rising RS = outperforming, even on a down week.
- **Moving averages (20/50/200-day):** the trend lines. A healthy uptrend has price above a rising 20, itself above the 200. "Extended" means far above the 20-day and prone to a pullback.
- **Breadth:** how many stocks participate. 50% above the 200-day and 0% above the 50-day means a small group is holding the index up while most stocks weaken.
- **VIX:** the fear gauge. Under 20 is calm.
- **Credit spreads:** extra yield on risky corporate bonds over safe government bonds. Widening warns of stress; tight means calm. Marked n/a this week.
- **High-yield / junk bonds (HYG):** riskier-company bonds. Investors sell them first when scared, so a falling price is an early credit warning.
- **Safe havens (gold, the dollar):** where money runs when afraid. Both flat-to-soft this week says fear stayed low.
- **Treasury yields / 2s10s:** the 10-year minus the 2-year. Positive and steepening (43bp here) with rising yields signals a hotter growth/inflation read — a headwind for expensive growth stocks.
