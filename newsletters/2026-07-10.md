# The Money Didn't Leave, It Moved

*Weekly Market Newsletter — Issue for the week ending Friday, July 10, 2026*

Oil ripped higher on a fresh Middle East flare-up, and the Fed minutes told everyone waiting for a rate cut to stop waiting. Under both of those, the S&P 500 still closed up 1.2% on the week. The story isn't the index. It's where the cash went while the headlines screamed: into refiners, hospitals, and the firms that manage other people's money, and out of farm equipment and cement. Our read rests on ranking about 1,900 US stocks by relative strength every day, and this week the board reshuffled hard.

## 1. Oil is back, and the refiners are eating

The renewed US-Iran fight put shipping near a standstill in the Strait of Hormuz again, the US revoked Iran's oil-sanctions relief after tanker attacks, and Brent crude jumped 5.9% on the week. When crude spikes on a supply scare rather than booming demand, the winners aren't the drillers first. They're the refiners, who turn crude into gasoline and diesel and pocket a fatter margin when product prices run ahead of the barrel.

Our board caught it in real time. Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing surged from #65 to #13 as a peer group, and Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation — the pipelines and tank farms that get more valuable when the Strait chokes — climbed from #99 to #51. You can see it in individual names too: DINO jumped 738 spots to #356 and MPC climbed 706 to #371 on the week.

The catch worth saying out loud: this move is riding a geopolitical headline, and OPEC+ is adding 188,000 barrels a day from August. If the Strait reopens and that supply lands, the crude-price support behind these refiners can drain as fast as it filled. Respect the strength while the trend holds; don't chase a vertical chart after it's already run.

## 2. The Fed shut the door, and yields noticed

The June minutes, and Chair Warsh's first meeting, landed on the same message: no rate cuts this year, and with a fresh oil shock feeding inflation worries, the market is now pricing in the odds of a *hike* rather than a cut. That's a real shift from the "cuts are coming" mood that carried stocks earlier in the year.

The bond market moved accordingly. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 4.54%, the 2-year added 2 to 4.16%, and the 2s10s spread sits at a positive 38bp, steepening. A rising long yield is a tax on expensive, long-duration stocks — the chips and richly-valued growth names whose value sits years out in the future. It's part of why money keeps favoring cash-generating, near-term businesses like refiners, insurers, and hospital operators over the priciest growth right now.

Inflation is still running at 4.2% year over year, unemployment is 4.2%, and the last payrolls print was a soft +57k. Sticky prices, a cooling-but-not-cracking job market, a Fed that won't ease: that's why next week's jobless claims (Thursday) matter more than usual. If claims start creeping up, the "no cuts" stance gets harder to hold.

And money left two groups hard. Agricultural & Farm Machinery fell from #23 to #103 on the board, and Construction Materials dropped from #46 to #109. ## 5. Theme of the week: reading, writing, and catching disease in your blood

This week's teaching spotlight is genomics and precision medicine — medicine rebuilt around your DNA. It's a curated look at one value chain, not a call on the whole market, so treat it as a lesson in how the plumbing works.

Picture your genome as a vast library. Five different businesses work on that library, and the money flows to whichever one is the chokepoint at the moment.

- **Sequencing** is reading the books. Illumina, PacBio, and 10x Genomics build the machines that spell out your DNA letter by letter. Node momentum 84 out of 100 — strong.
- **Tools** are the ink, paper, and lab reagents everyone else buys — Thermo Fisher, Agilent, Qiagen, Revvity. Momentum 76. A steady toll booth on the whole industry.
- **Diagnostics** is the early-warning blood test — a liquid biopsy that spots cancer or a genetic risk before symptoms show. Guardant, Natera, Tempus. This is the hottest link on the board at 87.
- **Gene Editing** is rewriting the sentences: Beam, CRISPR, Intellia, Editas. Momentum 69, the coolest link — the science is furthest from revenue, so it lags when rates are high.
- **AI Discovery** is the drafting assistant, using models to design drugs faster: Recursion, Schrödinger, Absci. Momentum 76.

The whole theme is steady, 76 now versus 77 a week ago, so nothing's breaking. What matters is *where inside the chain* the money sits: in diagnostics, the link closest to an actual paying customer and a repeatable test. That fits the broader market mood — cash favors the business that gets paid now over the one promising a cure in 2032.

The standout is Natera, top of the theme at 92 momentum and #42 on our full board, and it shows up on this week's watchlist too, which is the kind of cross-confirmation worth a second look on your own. For contrast, Beam screens as the cheapest on growth-adjusted sales (price-to-sales of 20.7 against 325% revenue growth), but it lives in the gene-editing link that's lagging, which is why "cheap" and "in favor" aren't the same thing. Do your own homework here; theme momentum is a starting point, not a verdict.

## 9. The Algo

The paper strategy sits at $51,060, up 2.1% since it started on May 27, versus the S&P's +0.7% and QQQ's -0.5% over the same stretch. It gave back 1.1% on the week and is 3.8% off its peak, running 20 open positions against 29 closed. The full record is honest and unflattering: 29 closed trades, 8 winners, a 28% win rate, and an average of -0.7% per trade. Small winners let run, lots of small stops — that's what a trend system looks like early, before a few big winners carry the whole thing.

It closed four this week, all stops, all losers: CINF (-4.2%), ECPG (-6.1%), UAL (-8.3%), and ALGT (-10.2%). The two airlines getting stopped fits the tape: Delta reported and fell 6% on the week, and airlines have taken analyst downgrades. The open book leans hard into the rotation this letter keeps describing. It's stuffed with financials (BAC, C, MET, USB, AXP, JPM and more) and health care (DVA up 9.6%, CVS, BTSG, PGNY, ADUS), with DaVita and BNY Mellon the biggest unrealized winners. New entries this week — PGNY, BTSG, ADUS, FTNT — were all bought right into the health-care and security strength the board is flagging. A clean illustration of buying strength early, executed by rules and stopped out mechanically when wrong.

*Methodology. The Algo is a long-only, rules-based paper strategy on a $50,000 notional sleeve, trading the same ~1,900-stock universe. A name is bought only if it clears every gate: its sector ranks near the top of the board with broad participation, earnings are growing year-over-year in both revenue and profit, it isn't already held, and it's past a 21-day cooldown. Qualifying setups are sorted into four conviction tiers by how many independent signals fire — from T1 (multiple 'strong' chart patterns, risked at 1.5% of the sleeve) down to T4 (relative-strength acceleration, 0.5%). Size is set by risk, not dollars: each trade is sized so a stop 2×ATR below entry costs only that tier's risk budget, capped at $2,000–$6,000. Exits are mechanical — scale out a third at +2R and a third at +4R (1R = the initial stop distance), trail the remainder by 2×ATR, book some after a +25% spike, and time out positions left flat after 25 trading days. New entries pause if the book is down 2% on the day or 8% from its peak. Signals are generated after the close and filled the next session at the live price plus 0.15% slippage. These are educational paper results — not advice, and not a live account.*

## 11. Glossary

- **Our rank / the board:** we rank every US stock we track by relative strength, #1 strongest. "#8 in our system" means 8th-strongest of about 1,900.
- **Relative strength (RS):** how a stock performs against the rest of the market, not on its own. Rising RS = outperforming.
- **Moving averages (20/50/200-day):** the average price over that many days — the trend lines. A healthy uptrend has price above a rising 20-day, itself above the 200-day. "Extended" means far above the 20-day and prone to a pullback.
- **VIX:** the market's fear gauge. Below 20 is calm, above 20 is stress.
- **Credit spreads:** the extra yield investors demand to hold risky corporate bonds over safe government bonds. Widening is an early warning; tight is calm.
- **Treasury yields / 2s10s:** the interest rate the US government pays to borrow for 10 years versus 2 years. The 2s10s spread is the 10-year minus the 2-year; a rising 10-year pressures expensive, long-duration stocks like chips and growth.
- **Breadth:** how many stocks take part. Strong breadth (most stocks above their 200-day, new highs beating new lows) means a broad, healthy rally.
