# Cargo Trucks, Hotel Rooms, and an Oil Spike Nobody Panicked About

*Issue — Friday, 2026-09-04. A weekly read built on ranking ~1,900 US stocks by relative strength every day.*

Brent crude jumped almost 8% on the week as fighting flared between the US and Iran, the kind of headline that usually drags the whole tape down with it. This time the S&P closed at 7,718.6, up a whisker (+0.1%) and sitting just 1.0% under its August 13 high. The fear gauges never woke up. Underneath that quiet surface, the board did plenty of moving — money left expensive software and crowded into truckers, utilities, hotels, apparel and auto parts.

## 1. The oil scare that stayed in its lane

The lead story of the week came from the Middle East. US–Iran fighting renewed supply-disruption fears, and Brent ran +7.8% over the week to 96.28. That's a real move, and it showed up on our board: energy names climbed hard, with APA at #15 and Antero at #16 among the strongest, and Archrock vaulting 1,693 spots into the top 200.

What separates a scare from a crisis is whether the fear bleeds into anything else. It didn't. The VIX sat at 14.52, asleep. Gold, the oldest panic asset, went nowhere (-0.0% on the week). Junk bonds barely twitched (HYG -0.2%). The dollar, which bids up when money runs for cover, actually softened (-0.5%). Four separate fear channels, four shrugs.

So the read is simple: an oil-supply story, contained to oil, not a broad risk-off. If gold had ripped and junk had cracked alongside crude, I'd be writing a very different issue. For now the spike is a tax on drivers and airlines and a tailwind for energy producers. Watch it, because a contained fire can jump the line, but don't confuse one loud gauge for five.

## 5. Theme of the Week: the AI value chain, and where the money actually sits

Think of the AI build-out as a river running from raw materials up to the software you actually use. At the source: the specialty chemicals, gases and materials that make a chip possible. Downstream, the tools that etch the chips, the chips themselves, the memory that feeds them, the networking gear that lashes them together, the power and cooling that keep them alive, and finally the clouds and apps that rent it all out.

Money flows to the chokepoint, the link everyone needs and few can supply, and cost flows away from it. Right now the whole river is running cooler: our AI theme's median momentum slipped to 37 from 40 a week ago. The interesting part is *where* the money is pooling inside the chain.

The hottest link isn't the glamorous one. It's Materials — the chemicals-and-gases layer (APD, Linde, DuPont, Entegris, MKS) — running a 64/100 node score, far ahead of everything downstream. Chip Equipment is at 22, AI Chips at 34, Design & IP at 34. In plain terms, the market is paying up for the shovels' raw metal while cooling on the shovels and the diggers. That happens when investors want AI exposure but don't trust the near-term chip cycle, so they retreat one step upstream to the suppliers who get paid no matter which chipmaker wins.

Two names to know from the chain, as education, not a nudge:
- Networking's Arista Networks (ANET) carries the strongest single momentum in the theme at 68/100 and ranks #54 on our board — the healthiest link above the materials layer.
- On a pure cheapness screen (again, a screen, not advice), Supermicro (SMCI) shows a price-to-sales of 0.5 against 123% year-over-year revenue growth. That combination is rare, and it usually means the market is pricing in either a blow-up or a collapse in margins. Do the homework before you decide which.

One footnote worth its own line: Broadcom reported this week, and despite what the headlines called a 221% surge in AI revenue, the stock fell 3% on the week as guidance underwhelmed. Macquarie still upgraded it (Neutral to Outperform). A monster growth number that the stock sells anyway — that's the AI chip cycle in a sentence right now.

*Methodology. The Algo is a long-only, rules-based paper strategy on a $50,000 notional sleeve, trading the same ~1,900-stock universe. A name is bought only if it clears every gate: its sector ranks near the top of the board with broad participation, earnings are growing year-over-year in both revenue and profit, it isn't already held, and it's past a 21-day cooldown. Qualifying setups are sorted into four conviction tiers by how many independent signals fire — from T1 (multiple 'strong' chart patterns, risked at 1.5% of the sleeve) down to T4 (relative-strength acceleration, 0.5%). Size is set by risk, not dollars: each trade is sized so a stop 2×ATR below entry costs only that tier's risk budget, capped at $2,000–$6,000. New entries pause if the book is down 2% on the day or 8% from its peak. Signals are generated after the close and filled the next session at the live price plus 0.15% slippage. These are educational paper results — not advice, and not a live account.*

## 11. Glossary

- **Our rank / the board:** we rank every US stock we track by relative strength, #1 strongest. "#8 in our system" means 8th-strongest of ~1,900.
- **Relative strength (RS):** performance versus the rest of the market, not in isolation. Rising RS = outperforming.
- **Moving averages (20/50/200-day):** the trend lines. A healthy uptrend has price above a rising 20-day, itself above the 200-day. "Extended" means far above the 20-day and prone to a pullback.
- **VIX:** the fear gauge. Below 20 = calm, above 20 = stress.
- **Credit spreads:** the extra yield investors demand on risky corporate bonds over safe government bonds. Widening = early warning; tight = calm.
- **Treasury yields / 2s10s:** the 10-year minus the 2-year. A rising 10-year yield pressures expensive, long-duration stocks; a negative ("inverted") spread has historically warned of recession.
- **Breadth:** how many stocks participate. Most stocks above their 200-day and new highs beating new lows = a broad, healthy rally.
- **Junk bonds:** bonds from riskier companies. Investors sell them first when scared, so a falling junk price is an early credit-stress signal.
- **Safe havens (gold, the dollar):** where money runs when afraid. A rally in either during a shock means the fear is real; flat through a scary headline means the market shrugged.
- **Node momentum:** the platform's 0-100 composite for a value-chain link (RS level and trend, EMA slopes, price vs the 50-day). 50 is the middle of the universe.
